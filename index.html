About The Tired Hippy Co

I'm the Tired Hippy, a creator from Western Washington. I work primarily with wood, but I'm always experimenting with different materials and ideas. I make wooden pendants, dab tools, bowl pokers, my own wood finish, and hand-dyed shirts and bandanas. I primarily sell on my Etsy, but you can also contact me for customs or special requests. Pretty much everything I make starts with the same idea: make something genuinely cool that I'd actually want to use myself.

I started woodworking with my grandfather when I was very young. I still have a project I made with him that has the date written on it: I was four years old at the time. I spent years lending a hand and working on projects with him, and somewhere along the way I fell in love with making things myself. When he passed, I inherited some of his tools. Since then I've added plenty of my own, and I've continued building on what he taught me.

My grandfather loved making things for other people. He enjoyed watching someone pick up something he'd made, inspect it, and appreciate all the little details. I realized I feel the exact same rush. His work was rustic and practical; mine tends to be more refined and sculptural, but the fundamentals he taught me are still there. I took those lessons, added my own style, and kept building from there.

The Tired Hippy Co grew out of that process. What started with experimenting with dab tools eventually grew into something that I thought could become a real business. I didn't want to sell things I wouldn't use myself, so I spent years refining products that became part of my own everyday life. I haven't used a conventional dab tool in more than six years. I've worn my own pendants every day for years, and most of what I wear is something I've dyed myself.

I don't try to make every piece look the same. I like experimenting with different shapes, designs, and woods, including exotic species, and letting each piece develop its own character. Every piece is worked on individually, from selecting the wood blank to carving, sanding, finishing, and crafting the tip. It's a one-of-a-kind piece that was made for someone—even if I didn't know who that someone was yet.
